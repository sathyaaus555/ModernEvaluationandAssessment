02 · Classify the use case

Three responsible choices

ClassificationAssessment example · adaptedAction
Fully appropriateDraft practice MCQs from an approved syllabus.Use AI for a draft, with routine educator checking.
Appropriate with human reviewSuggest feedback on a student's essay.Educator checks accuracy, fairness and suitability before sharing.
InappropriateAllow AI alone to make a final, high-stakes student outcome decision without meaningful human oversight.Keep final professional decision with the authorised human.

Source concept: The classification depends on the four criteria together; the most decisive factor is called the load-bearing criterion. The education examples above are training adaptations.

03 · Human review gate

“Human in the loop” is not enough

The course requires a review gate with three specific answers:

WHOWho reviews?The responsible teacher or authorised assessor
WHATWhat do they verify?Accuracy, fairness, outcome alignment and policy compliance
WHENWhen?Before feedback, marks or decisions are released
04 · Privacy and protection

Classify data before uploading

Green · Safe to use

Published, anonymised, aggregated or approved materials. Still follow organisational policy.

Yellow · Review first

Internal documents, personal contact details or confidential drafts. Confirm permission before use.

Red · Keep out unless approved

Regulated information, credentials, secrets or third-party confidential material. Use only an approved entry point.

Important: Incognito or memory controls do not replace permission to upload data. Redaction can help, but removing names alone may leave people identifiable.
05 · Fairness, policy and tools

Make responsibility a daily habit

Review for bias & fairness

Check whether an AI-generated question, example, feedback or marking criterion could disadvantage particular learners. Compare with intended learning outcomes and professional standards.

Check tools and permissions

Before enabling an AI Skill, connector or integration, check Source, Reach and Appropriateness. Use only the access needed for the task (least privilege).

Takeaway

Our responsible AI checklist

  1. Decide: Is AI appropriate for this assessment task?
  2. Protect: Is the learner data safe and approved for this tool?
  3. Review: Who checks what, and when?
  4. Verify: Are the output and marking decisions accurate and fair?
  5. Own: Keep responsibility with the authorised human.
