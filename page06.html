01 · Diagnose, don't guess

Four failure patterns

The course asks us to notice when the output went wrong, not just what is wrong.

Questions become less consistent after a long conversationContext overloadRestart with a focused summary

03 · Live demonstration

Improve the question, not just the wording

Click through the diagnosis, revised instruction and improved MCQ.

Diagnosis: Under-specification

The prompt “Create an MCQ about assessment” does not specify the learner level, learning outcome, cognitive demand, answer quality or response format.

Specific critique: “The item does not assess whether learners can distinguish formative from summative assessment.”

Turn the reaction into an instruction

Unhelpful reaction: “Make the question better.”
Specific revised prompt:

“Create one scenario-based MCQ for trainee teachers that assesses the ability to identify formative assessment. Use a realistic classroom scenario, four plausible but mutually distinct options, one correct answer, and a brief explanation. Avoid ‘all of the above.’”

Revised MCQ · illustrative output

A teacher gives students a short quiz halfway through a unit, reviews the responses, and reteaches a concept many students misunderstood. What is the main purpose of the quiz?

A. To rank students for the final report
B. To adjust teaching based on evidence of learning
C. To certify completion of the entire course
D. To replace all classroom observations

Correct answer: B. This is formative use of assessment evidence to improve learning.

Check the fix: Does it now measure the intended outcome? Are options distinct and the answer defensible? Human review is still required.

04 · Feedback loop

Turn reactions into instructions

Reaction

“Too generic.”
“Missed the point.”
“The distractors are weak.”

Specific adjustment

State the audience and learning outcome.
Define the exact question to assess.
Require plausible, non-overlapping distractors.

Course principle: Identify which lever to change: the prompt, loaded context or configuration. Then test whether the change actually improved the output.

05 · Promote the fix

Make improvements repeatable

When a correction will be needed again, save it in a reusable standing instruction or Skill, rather than repeating the same manual feedback every time.

Example · reusable assessment instruction

“For every MCQ, specify the learning outcome, use one unambiguously correct answer, write plausible distinct distractors, include the answer rationale, and flag any content requiring subject-matter verification.”

Workflow: Feedback → Identify problem → Specific adjustment → Test the fix → Promote to configuration.
