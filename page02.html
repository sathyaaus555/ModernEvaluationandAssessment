Topic 02 · AI for assessment design

Understanding AI for Effective Assessment Design

8 MIN

Generative AI can help educators draft questions, scenarios, rubrics and feedback. Understanding how it works — and where it can fail — helps faculty use it responsibly.

What AI can and cannot do

AI can assist

Generate first drafts of MCQs, case studies, rubrics, model answers and differentiated practice tasks.

AI cannot guarantee

Factual correctness, alignment with a syllabus, fair wording or valid marking criteria without educator review.

Four AI capabilities · Assessment examples

01 · Next-token prediction

How AI generates responses

Language models produce text by predicting likely next tokens from context. Fluent answers are not proof of correctness.

Assessment example: AI drafts a multiple-choice question and answer key.
Limitation: It may confidently choose an incorrect answer.
Educator action: Verify the answer and explanation.
02 · Knowledge

What AI can draw upon

AI can use patterns learned during training and information provided in the prompt, but its knowledge can be incomplete or outdated.

Assessment example: AI suggests questions for a course topic.
Limitation: It may invent facts or use content outside the syllabus.
Educator action: Check against approved course material.
03 · Working memory

What AI can use in the current context

The model uses the instructions and materials available within its context window; long or unclear inputs may cause details to be missed.

Assessment example: Provide learning outcomes, a topic outline and marking criteria.
Limitation: The model may overlook a constraint in a long prompt.
Educator action: Keep requirements explicit and check every output.
04 · Steerability

How educators guide AI output

Clear instructions can influence the format, difficulty, cognitive level and style of generated questions.

Assessment example: Request three Apply-level questions aligned to a specified outcome.
Limitation: AI may still produce questions at the wrong level.
Educator action: Review alignment with Bloom's taxonomy.

One practical example · From a vague prompt to an aligned task

Vague prompt“Generate five questions on Python loops.”

The output may mix recall questions, unrelated topics and inconsistent difficulty.

Better assessment prompt“For first-year students, create three Apply-level questions on Python for-loops. Align them with the outcome ‘write loops to process a list’. Include an answer key and brief marking criteria. Do not use topics outside the provided syllabus.”

The educator still checks correctness, cognitive level and suitability.

Human review · The essential safeguard

01Check accuracyAre the question and answer correct?
02Check alignmentDoes it assess the intended outcome?
03Check fairnessIs it clear, inclusive and accessible?
04ApproveEducator makes the final decision.
Key takeaway: AI is a drafting partner, not an autonomous assessor. Effective assessment still depends on educators defining learning outcomes and validating the results.
