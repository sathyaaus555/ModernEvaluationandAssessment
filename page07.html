Activity · 4 minutes

A poorly designed AI-generated MCQ

Question: What is the best teaching method?

A. Lectures
B. Group discussions
C. Technology
D. All of the above

AI answer: D

Your mission: Identify two problems, rewrite the prompt, and decide how you would verify the revised question.

  1. Diagnose: What makes this question weak or unfair?
  2. Improve: What learning outcome, scenario, audience and constraints are missing?
  3. Verify: How will you check the answer and distractors before using it?

Timer runs in this browser tab. No responses are collected or submitted.

Example diagnosis

The question has no stated learning outcome, “best” depends on context, the options are not comparable, and “all of the above” is a weak shortcut.

Improved prompt

Act as an assessment designer. Create one scenario-based MCQ for trainee teachers assessing their ability to choose a formative assessment technique. Use a realistic classroom situation, four plausible and distinct options, exactly one defensible correct answer, and a short rationale. Avoid “all of the above” and unsupported universal claims.

Example revised MCQ

A teacher wants to identify misconceptions midway through a lesson so that they can adjust the next activity. Which approach best meets this goal?

A. Give a final exam after the unit
B. Use an exit ticket and review responses before the next lesson
C. Rank students by attendance
D. Award a grade for classroom participation only

Answer: B. Exit tickets can provide immediate evidence to guide instruction.

Human verification: Check alignment with the stated learning outcome, subject accuracy, clarity, accessibility, distractor plausibility and marking rationale.

Live discussion

Audience poll · 2 minutes

What is the most important first step when an AI-generated assessment question is poor?

Best answer: B. The troubleshooting sequence starts with the cheapest fix: check whether the prompt specifies the task, context, constraints and output format. Then check context, tool/model, configuration and task fitness.

This is a local self-check, not a live multi-person vote. For a group-wide poll, invite a show of hands or use your institution's polling tool.

Open floor · 3 minutes

Q&A prompts

Invite audience questions, or open one of these discussion starters.

Can we use an AI-generated question directly in an exam?

Use AI output as a draft. An authorised educator should verify syllabus alignment, accuracy, fairness, accessibility, answer keys and institutional policy before release—especially for high-stakes assessment.

What if the question is technically correct but too easy?

Specify the intended cognitive level and learning outcome. Request a scenario or application task, then check that the item genuinely assesses that level rather than merely using harder wording.

What if AI keeps producing the same weak distractors?

Give specific feedback: require plausible, distinct alternatives based on common misconceptions. Test the revision, and save the effective instruction for recurring work.

Can we upload student answers or marks for AI feedback?

Only when organisational policy, privacy requirements and the approved tool permit it. Assess data sensitivity, remove identifiers where appropriate, and retain meaningful human review.

Close · 1 minute

Three things to remember

01 · Design

Give AI a clear task, learning outcome, context and format.

02 · Verify

Review accuracy, completeness, fitness, fairness and privacy.

03 · Improve

Diagnose weak output, make a specific change and reuse proven fixes.

Closing question: “What is one change you will make to your next assessment after today's session?”
