Topic 04 · Review before use

Evaluating AI Output

10 MIN

An AI-generated assessment can look polished and still be inaccurate, incomplete, unsuitable or unfair. Evaluate against evidence, not appearance.

Why evaluation matters in modern assessment

Imagine: AI generates five MCQs, a scenario question and a marking rubric. The language is clear, but one answer key is wrong and one important learning outcome is missing.

Question for the audience: “Would you use these questions in an actual assessment without reviewing them?”

Discernment · Three checks for AI-generated assessments

Module 3 defines Discernment as reviewing output against requirements, source material and professional standards, rather than judging how convincing it looks.

01 · Accuracy

Is it correct? Verify facts, calculations, model answers and answer keys against reliable course material.

Assessment example: AI marks an incorrect Python loop as the correct MCQ option. Correct the key before use.

02 · Completeness

Is anything important missing? Check every requested learning outcome, question, criterion and explanation.

Assessment example: Five questions are supplied, but none assesses the specified Apply-level outcome.

03 · Fitness for Purpose

Does it work for the intended audience and goal? Review learner level, clarity, difficulty and whether the task measures the desired skill.

Assessment example: A technically correct question is too advanced for first-year students.
Key distinction: Accuracy ≠ Completeness. Every statement may be correct while a crucial requirement is still missing.

Recognise common failure patterns

Hallucinations

AI may generate plausible but unsupported claims, fabricated details or confident answers without evidence.

Assessment example: An invented research citation is presented as the source for a question.

Action: Verify references, statistics, answer keys and quotations using actual sources.

Bias

AI output can reflect a one-sided framing or reinforce an assumption built into the prompt.

Assessment example: A question assumes one viewpoint is correct without allowing evidence-based alternatives.

Action: Check balanced wording, inclusivity and whether students can demonstrate learning fairly.

Also check · Inconsistencies

The answer key, explanation and rubric may contradict one another, even within the same polished output. Cross-check all parts before publishing.

Evaluate using three fixed references

01RequirementsLearning outcomes, level, assessment instructions
02SourcesApproved syllabus, teaching notes, reliable references
03StandardsMarking quality, fairness, clarity and accessibility

Choose a review verdict

Ready to Use

Meets requirements, is supported by sources and fits the purpose.

Needs Revision

Usable direction, but a correctable error or omission remains.

Needs Human Override

Unverified, high-stakes or seriously unsuitable output requires human judgment before proceeding.

Stakes matter: High-stakes assessment decisions need more rigorous verification than low-stakes practice drafts. Educators retain final responsibility.

Fact-checking · Build verification into the prompt

“Using only the attached syllabus and learning outcomes, review these assessment questions. For each item, check factual accuracy, outcome coverage, intended cognitive level and suitability for first-year students. Flag unsupported claims or missing information. Do not invent references. Return a table with Issue, Evidence and Suggested Revision.”

Verification instructions can reduce unsupported output, but the AI's self-review is not a substitute for checking original sources and applying human judgment.

AI Fluency connection: Discernment is how we review AI output; Diligence is why we verify and remain accountable. Judge the output against requirements, sources and standards, then decide whether to use, revise or override it.

Suggested 10-minute delivery

1 min · Opening assessment dilemma
3 min · Accuracy, Completeness and Fitness for Purpose
2 min · Hallucinations, Bias and Inconsistencies
2 min · Spot-the-problem example
2 min · Verification and review verdicts
