Topic 01 · Modern evaluation & assessment frameworks

Introduction to Modern Evaluation & Assessment

Modern assessment goes beyond measuring what students remember. It gathers evidence of what learners understand, can apply and can improve — using well-aligned tasks, feedback and, where appropriate, responsibly supervised AI tools.

Traditional vs Modern Assessment

Traditional assessment

Assessment mainly of learning

Traditional assessment mainly focuses on testing what students have learned, often through examinations and marks. It primarily measures achievement after learning.

Modern assessment

Assessment of, for and as learning

Modern assessment looks at both achievement and improvement. It combines examinations with practical activities, projects, ongoing feedback and opportunities for students to apply and improve their learning.

Three approaches used together

✓
During learning

Formative

Use evidence to identify gaps and improve learning while there is time to act.

Example: A short quiz followed by targeted feedback.
★
Judging achievement

Summative

Evaluate achievement against learning outcomes, often at a defined endpoint.

Example: A final examination or graded project.
↻
Across the course

Continuous

Collect learning evidence regularly through multiple opportunities.

Example: Weekly labs, quizzes and project milestones.

Formative and summative refer primarily to purpose; continuous describes how assessment is organised over time. They can overlap.

Educational foundations · Why assessment must be designed well

John Biggs · 1996

Constructive Alignment

Align learning outcomes → learning activities → assessment tasks. Assess the skills students were expected to develop.

Example: If the outcome is to write code, assess a coding task — not only definitions.
Anderson & Krathwohl (eds.) · 2001

Revised Bloom's Taxonomy

Match assessment tasks to cognitive demand: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyse, Evaluate, Create.

Example: “Define a loop” assesses different thinking from “Write a loop to solve a problem.”

Feedback foundation: Black & Wiliam (1998) and Sadler (1989) explain why assessment evidence and feedback should help learners improve.

AI-enhanced assessment · The contemporary approach

Recent guidance: UNESCO's AI Competency Framework for Teachers (Miao & Cukurova, 2024) emphasises human-centred use, ethics, AI pedagogy and teacher competence. The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 examines AI's opportunities and challenges in education. These are contemporary guidance sources — not replacements for educational assessment theory.

Where AI can assist
  • Draft MCQs, case studies and authentic scenarios aligned to outcomes.
  • Suggest questions at different Bloom's levels.
  • Draft marking rubrics and actionable feedback.
  • Suggest differentiated practice for learning gaps.
  • Help educators revise weak or ambiguous questions.
Where educators remain accountable
  • Check factual accuracy and intended answers.
  • Verify alignment with outcomes and cognitive level.
  • Review bias, accessibility and cultural fairness.
  • Protect student data and follow institutional policy.
  • Approve final tasks, rubrics and consequential judgements.

Our practical framework · AI-Assisted Assessment Lifecycle

This six-step presentation workflow synthesises established assessment design principles and responsible AI guidance. It is our instructional model, not an official UNESCO or OECD named framework.

01DefineSpecify learning outcomes.
02DesignChoose tasks and success criteria.
03GenerateUse AI to draft questions or rubrics.
04EvaluateCheck accuracy, alignment and fairness.
05ApproveEducator reviews and finalises.
06ImproveUse feedback and evidence to refine.

One classroom example · Python loops

Exam-centred exampleEnd-of-unit test

Students answer loop questions after several weeks. A final grade reveals achievement, but misconceptions may be identified too late to help during the unit.

Modern, AI-supported examplePractice → Feedback → Improvement

The lecturer checks understanding after the lesson, uses an AI-drafted practice task aligned to Apply, verifies its answer and rubric, provides feedback, and checks progress again.
