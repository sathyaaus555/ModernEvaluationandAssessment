Topic 01 · Educational assessment frameworks

Introduction to Modern Evaluation & Assessment

5 MIN

Modern evaluation and assessment use evidence of student learning to make informed decisions: what learners have achieved, where they need support, and how teaching and assessment can improve.

From marks to meaningful evidence

Predominantly traditional emphasis

Assessment of learning

Evaluates achievement against expected standards, often at the end of a course or unit.

  • End-of-unit tests and final examinations
  • Grades and certification of achievement
  • Often answers: What has the student achieved?
Modern complementary emphasis

Assessment for learning

Uses evidence during instruction to improve student learning and teaching decisions.

  • Regular checks for understanding
  • Timely, actionable feedback
  • Answers: What should the learner do next?
Key distinction: Modern assessment does not replace examinations. Effective assessment combines evidence of achievement with feedback that improves learning. Research foundation: Paul Black & Dylan Wiliam (1998); D. Royce Sadler (1989).

Assessment approaches: purpose and timing

✓
Improve learning

Formative assessment

Evidence is interpreted and used to adjust teaching or learning while progress is still possible.

Example: A short quiz reveals misconceptions; the lecturer explains the difficult concept again.
Research: Black & Wiliam (1998).
★
Judge achievement

Summative assessment

Evidence is used to judge achievement against learning outcomes, commonly at the end of a unit or course.

Example: A final examination or graded capstone project demonstrates mastery.
↻
Monitor over time

Continuous assessment

Progress is evaluated through repeated tasks and evidence collected throughout a course.

Example: Weekly quizzes, assignments, lab work and project milestones.

Note: Formative and summative describe the purpose of assessment; continuous describes its frequency and organisation. A continuous assessment task may serve either purpose.

Framework 1 · Constructive Alignment

Author: John Biggs (1996). Assessment tasks and teaching activities should align with the intended learning outcomes. Students should be assessed on what they were expected to learn.

01Learning outcomesWhat should students be able to demonstrate?
02Learning activitiesWhat practice helps them develop the skill?
03Aligned assessmentWhat task produces valid evidence of the skill?
04Feedback & revisionHow can evidence guide improvement?

The first three elements express Biggs's constructive alignment; feedback and revision are included here as an extension to illustrate ongoing assessment practice.

Framework 2 · Revised Bloom's Taxonomy

Authors: Lorin W. Anderson & David R. Krathwohl (editors, 2001), with collaborators. A cognitive framework for choosing the depth of learning required by an assessment task.

RememberRecall key facts
UnderstandExplain concepts
ApplyUse knowledge
AnalyseExamine relationships
EvaluateJustify a judgement
CreateProduce a new solution
Remember-level questionWhat is a Python for-loop?

Checks recall or basic understanding, but does not demonstrate programming performance.

Apply-level assessmentWrite a Python loop to calculate the sum of five values.

Requires students to apply the concept and provides evidence of a practical learning outcome.

Framework 3 · Formative Assessment & Feedback

Key researchers: Paul Black & Dylan Wiliam (1998); D. Royce Sadler (1989). Use evidence of current understanding, clarify the expected standard, provide feedback, and give learners an opportunity to close the gap.

01Clarify successShare criteria and learning goals.
02Elicit evidenceUse quizzes, discussion or tasks.
03Provide feedbackIdentify strengths and gaps.
04Act on feedbackRevise teaching and student work.

Connecting assessment frameworks to AI

Learning outcome: Students can apply Python loops to solve a simple problem.

Constructive Alignment: Assess students through a coding task, not only a definition question.

Bloom's Taxonomy: Target the Apply cognitive level.

Formative Assessment: Review common mistakes and provide opportunities to improve.

AI's supporting role: Suggest questions, rubrics and feedback drafts; the educator validates accuracy, alignment, fairness and suitability before use.
